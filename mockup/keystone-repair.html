It reads as a cup because its numbers are a cup's numbers

Not a matter of taste. A keystone and a tumbler have almost identical taper, so the slant was never the problem and adjusting it would never have helped. Two other properties do the discriminating, and the current mark is on the wrong side of both.

Three repairs

Each keeps the keystone idea, the stone that holds the span, and attacks a different one of the four failing properties. Shown at the sizes the mark actually ships at, because 16px is where marks die.

Does it still read as a keystone?

The honest test. A keystone only means anything seated in its arch, and the logo is never seen in an arch. If the repair only works with the arch drawn around it, the repair has not worked.

What I would actually do

Take C. It fixes all four properties at once and it is the only one where the cup reading is structurally impossible rather than merely weakened. A shape with no flat base cannot be a vessel, and a letter cut out of a surface cannot be contents. It is also the strongest of the three at 16px, because a knockout letter holds its counters when an inset one closes up.

But the larger point stands, and it is the one worth deciding. The original write-up flagged this exact risk: "without the story it is a trapezoid with a K in it." Your reaction is that prediction landing, unprompted, from the person who already knows the story. A mark that needs a sentence to work is a bet that you will always be in the room to say it, and on a website you are never in the room.

So the real question is not which repair. It is whether direction A is worth repairing at all when D is already live, costs nothing, and nobody has ever had to explain it. I would fix the drawing if you love the idea, and drop it without regret if you only liked it.
