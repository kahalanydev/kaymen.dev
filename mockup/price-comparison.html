K kaymen.dev “vs hiring someone” — three directions

Direction 1 of 3

The arithmetic show the sum, let them check it

The premise

The comparison people make in their head is “$1,800/mo vs a salary”, and they make it wrong, because a salary is not what an employee costs. So do the sum in public: base, payroll tax, benefits, equipment, recruiting amortised, and the months before anyone is productive. It sits directly under the plans, where the objection actually forms.

What changes

One new block on /#price, after .plans and before the .fine line. No new page, no new nav item. All the kaymen.dev figures already exist in index.html; only the hire side is new, and it is six numbers.

What it costs

It invites a spreadsheet fight. Anyone who has hired will have their own multiplier and will argue with yours, which turns a page about trust into a page about assumptions. And it anchors on price at the exact moment the site was winning on evidence. It also dates: salaries move, and a stale figure is worse than none.

Direction 2 of 3

What you're actually buying cost is one row, not the argument

The premise

Money is the easiest thing to compare and the least useful. The real differences are continuity when someone is away, breadth of stack, how long before anything ships, and what happens the day they resign. Put cost in the table as one row among seven — and concede two rows outright, because a comparison that wins every line is read as marketing.

What changes

A seven-row table under the plans. No arithmetic to defend, no salary figure to keep current, nothing that goes stale. It is also the only direction that needs zero new data — every claim is about how you already work.

What it costs

It does not answer the question asked. Someone who came to compare prices leaves without a number, and “it depends” reads as evasion to a buyer who has a budget line to fill. It is also the least quotable — nobody forwards a table.

Direction 3 of 3

The break-even including the part where you should not hire us

The premise

There is a real number of hours per month above which hiring is cheaper, and saying it out loud is the most persuasive thing on the page — because it is the only version where the reader can see you are not just selling. Drag the slider, get a straight answer, sometimes “hire someone”. It is No hostages applied to pricing.

What changes

One interactive block, about 40 lines of JS, no dependency. Same six hire-side numbers as direction A but they stay behind the slider instead of sitting in a table to be argued with — which quietly solves A's spreadsheet-fight problem.

What it costs

It tells some visitors to leave, and it will. It also needs the same figures A needs, so it is not less exposed to going stale — only less obviously. And an interactive widget on a page that is otherwise deliberately calm is the loudest thing on the site.
