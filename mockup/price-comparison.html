The premise

The first pass compared hiring us to maintain something against hiring a developer, which is a hypothetical about a future. This is a receipt. Six systems that are running right now, the real number of working days each one took, and what that same work would have been billed at elsewhere. Nobody has to believe a projection — the things are live and you can go and look at them.

Where the days come from

They are not estimates. content/stats.js is generated by scripts/refresh-stats.js out of real git history — distinct days carrying a commit, per project, per month. This section would be server-rendered from that file like the rest of the homepage, so the figures update themselves and cannot go stale the way a typed number does.

What it costs

Days-with-a-commit is a proxy, not a timesheet — a 20-minute fix and a 10-hour day both count as one. It reads low on thinking-heavy work and high on fiddly work, and somebody will say so. It also puts a number on projects whose clients could recognise themselves, so the anonymity interlock has to hold. And one row below goes against us.
