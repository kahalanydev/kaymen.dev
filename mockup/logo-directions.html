Five logo directions for kaymen.dev

Same palette, same typeface, same lock-up rules — the locked ones. What differs between these five is the idea, not the accent colour. Each is shown at the sizes and in the places it actually has to survive: the 224px rail, a 16px favicon, the dark email header, and the login screen.

First, the thing worth knowing: there are three marks in the tree today

They disagree with each other. The rail one is on-palette and fine. The braces are the pre-rebrand wordmark still sitting on the login screens. The favicon is the theme the site dropped on 15 August — it is linked from index.html:25, so it is on every browser tab right now.

The lineup — all five, three backgrounds, and the 16px test

In use — pick a direction

If you made me choose

B — Branch & merge. It is the only one of the five that says what the business actually sells. Kaymen.dev's offer is build and run: the site's whole spine is the running board, the fleet sparklines and the live-systems count. A K that is literally a trunk with a branch leaving and a line merging back is that promise, drawn. It is also the only one that reads as a mark rather than as a letter in a box.

Its honest cost: it is a git graph, and git graphs are not rare in developer tooling. It is distinctive against agencies, not against dev tools. And it is the most fragile of the five at 16px — the two nodes want to close up. That is a solvable drawing problem, not a concept problem, but it is real work, and D is already live and costs nothing.

The contrarian pick is A — Keystone. If you want a mark nobody else in this market has, it is that one: a keystone is the stone that holds the arch up, and pulling it collapses the span. That is the retainer argument in one shape. It needs the sentence to land, though — and a logo that needs a sentence is a bet on you being around to say it.
