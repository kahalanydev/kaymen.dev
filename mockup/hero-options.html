The first pass had the comparison backwards. "$29–80 per user" makes renting look cheap, because it prices one seat of one tool against a whole build. Nobody runs a business on one tool. The real number is the stack — five subscriptions that each do half a job, and the thing that actually hurts is not the price, it is that none of them talk to each other.

So the headline still says own it, the paragraph under it is the pain — tools that half-work, information scattered, nobody with one source of truth, and a bill that rises every time you hire — and the six blocks are the answer. They say what you get, not what the other lot withhold. Arguing against the competition immediately after making the offer reads as a second complaint; a buyer who has just been told the price wants to see what the price buys.

The panel is the argument in numbers. It is not "we are cheaper" — year one is not cheaper, and pretending otherwise would not survive a spreadsheet. It is you are already spending this, and at the end of it you own nothing.

What is live, and the seven things working against it

1. Every sentence is about you, not them. "We build it. Then we run it." The visitor's first question is "what do I get", and the answer on screen is your business model.

2. It is a category description, not a position. Any shop can write "we build and maintain". Nothing in it is hard to copy, so it cannot be owned.

3. "Run it" is ambiguous. Run my business? Run a server? It reads as hosting, which is the cheapest possible reading of what you actually do.

4. The lead is a capability list. "Multi-tenant platforms, hard integrations, apps in the stores" are inputs. Your own case-study page says it better: a capability list is free to write.

5. The best asset on the page is decoration. The Live fleet panel is real, generated from git, and almost nobody else can show one. It is parked in the right column while the headline says something generic.

6. It never names a buyer. Nothing on screen tells a three-person brokerage paying for Monday seats that this page is for them.

7. It no longer matches the next section. Pricing now opens with "still running on spreadsheets". The hero and the section under it are telling two different stories.
